In 2024 AI law was a timetable. In 2025 the first dates arrived.

From 2 February 2025, the EU AI Act's [prohibitions on certain AI practices and its AI literacy requirement applied](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/implementation-timeline/). The banned list in [Article 5](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/5/) covers practices such as manipulative techniques that cause harm and social scoring.

On 2 August 2025 the rules for general-purpose AI models began to apply [[1]](#source-1). To help providers meet them, the Commission published [the General-Purpose AI Code of Practice on 10 July 2025](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/contents-code-gpai), covering transparency, copyright and safety.

In the United States the picture stayed a patchwork. Colorado, the first state with a broad AI law, [pushed its requirements back to 30 June 2026](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb25b-004) in a bill approved in August 2025. Delays like this did not remove the work; they gave teams time to do it properly.

The lesson most companies took away was practical. Regulators and customers rarely ask "is your AI ethical?". They ask for documents: which systems you run, what data they use, who can override them, and what happened last time something went wrong. If those answers live in people's heads, compliance is a scramble every time.
